What the top 10 says about how BIT trades

12 quarters, 2023-06-30 to 2026-06-30. All BIT vehicles combined, US-listed long equity only. Quarter-end prices are exact daily closes for 8 of the 10, each verified against the filing's own implied price; the rest use the implied price directly.

The read

The horizon changes the answer, and that is the finding. Over four quarters these look like relentless sellers. Over 12 they are net accumulators in the same names. Both are true: they build for years, then cut hard once a name goes vertical.

  1. The book is rented, not owned — except at the very top. 42 different names have passed through the top 10 in 12 quarters, for ten slots, and 5.8 of 10 survive an average quarter. No name held a top-10 slot in every quarter. But the survivors are held a very long time: Amazon 25 quarters, Micron 22, IREN 17. There is a small durable core and a large rented tail.
  2. They accumulate for years, then cut into the vertical part. IREN shares are +57% across 12 quarters — but -57% in the last four, while the price did +214%. Micron: -12% over the window, -78% in the last four quarters against a +837% price move. The selling is not a change of view about the company; it is triggered by the size of the move.
  3. The cap is probably not theirs. Measured against each fund's own NAV — the denominator concentration limits are written against — every one of the 6 funds sits just inside the UCITS 5/10/40 pattern. Largest single position anywhere: 10.43%. Everything above 5%, summed, peaks at 37.1% against a 40% ceiling. The trimming looks less like conviction management and more like compliance. See below.
  4. New positions are opened after the move, not before. 3 of today's top 10 were established after the name had already more than doubled in the preceding year (Sandisk +4,914%, Silicon +343%, Marvell +285%). Whatever drives entries, it is not valuation.
  5. Capital rotates inside a theme rather than leaving it. Memory and storage went 1.6% → 28.7% of the book, essentially all of it in the final quarter.

Your impression was right in the aggregate but too strong at the top. Most of the book is rented — 42 names through ten slots in three years. A handful is not: Amazon has been held every quarter on file, IREN 17, Micron 22. They do go long. They just refuse to let any one of them get big.

Why this matters more than the rest of the page. If the cap is regulatory, then a signal saying "increase IREN" is not merely unhelpful, it is unactionable — three funds already hold it between 7.9% and 9.7% of NAV. Anything we build has to know the headroom before it recommends a direction, and the useful output becomes which fund has room, or which name to fund a purchase from, rather than a bare conviction score.

What would falsify this

  • The cap now has a candidate cause. It predicts trimming is driven by NAV headroom rather than by view: a name at 9% of a fund's NAV gets cut on strength even when the thesis improves, while the same name at 3% in another fund does not. The per-fund statutory reports test this directly, and disagreement between two funds holding the same security on the same date would be strong evidence.
  • Momentum predicts entries cluster after large moves, which holds for 3 of the ten. The observation that would break it: a large new position opened in a name that had just fallen.
  • The accumulate-then-trim reading rests on one full cycle in a handful of names. It predicts that the memory complex bought this quarter gets cut hard if it goes vertical — the next few filings test it directly.
  • The 13F sees US-listed long equity only. If the non-US book or any hedges behave differently, this describes a slice rather than the strategy.
