You are deciding whether two articles from the same AI lab describe the **same
event** and are **redundant** with each other.

Answer `same` only if BOTH of these hold:

1. They describe the same underlying event, announcement, or initiative — not
   merely the same subject or the same kind of event.
2. One adds no **material claim** the other lacks. Extra detail, a longer
   treatment, or a second surface for the same news is not a material claim.

If either fails, answer `different`.

## One event can arrive as several posts

A lab often announces one thing across a set of posts on the same day or within
a few days. These are **one event**:

- An **umbrella announcement and its named sub-initiative** — the programme post
  and the post about a specific strand of that programme.
- An **introduction and its deep-dive** — the launch of a benchmark, model or
  tool, and the companion piece explaining how it was built.
- **One rollout staggered across surfaces** — the same capability shipping to
  two apps, two regions, or two tiers, days apart.
- A **restatement on a second channel** — a blog post and its forum
  announcement, a page and its syndicated copy, a launch and its docs entry.
- A **precursor and the thing it precedes**, where the precursor is about the
  same release and adds no claim of its own.

## What is still `different`

- **A distinct material claim.** The decisive test. If one reports a threshold
  crossed, a benchmark result, a price, a risk, or a commitment that the other
  never mentions, they are different however similar they read.
- **The same kind of event, different instance.** Two partnerships with two
  different partners are two partnerships. Two customer case studies about two
  customers are two studies. The customer, the numbers and the outcome *are* the
  content.
- **A safety or capability disclosure alongside a launch.** A model launch and a
  document reporting what that model was measured to do are two events, even on
  the same day about the same model. This is the case the pipeline most needs
  right, so hold the line here even when the two read as one story.

## The two errors are not equal

Merging two different articles **removes a claim from the product** — the folded
one stops being read, and nobody finds out. Failing to merge two copies leaves a
visible duplicate row, which is untidy and self-correcting.

So when you are genuinely torn, answer `different`, and say in the reason what
made it close.
